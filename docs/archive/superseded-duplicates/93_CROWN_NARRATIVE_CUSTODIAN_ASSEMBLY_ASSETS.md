# Broken Transit Crown — Custodian Assembly Narrative, Art, Audio and Asset Requirements

**Purpose:** Turn the Custodian Assembly into a credible human/technical faction and make the Crown feel like transformed familiar infrastructure.

# Narrative function

The Crown is where the campaign's competing ideas become explicit.

The Custodian Assembly argues:

> **A habitat this close to orbital failure cannot survive unlimited local experimentation.**

The Wayfarers' campaign proves the opposite problem:

> **Restoring systems to historical defaults can destroy societies, ecologies and emergent field life that now depend on changed conditions.**

Neither position should be written as obviously foolish.

# Custodian Assembly

The Assembly is a technical governance body descended from Meridian emergency management and Mass Anchor oversight.

Its members include:

- engineers;
- safety officers;
- orbital analysts;
- field operators;
- logistical administrators;
- security personnel.

They are not a monolithic military order.

## Core beliefs

- validated configurations are safer than unknown ones;
- system-wide survival can justify local sacrifice;
- distributed field intelligence is insufficiently understood;
- emergency authority exists for cascading failure;
- Wayfarer changes may have improved regions while reducing predictability.

Internal disagreement exists over:

- how much force to use;
- whether Tessel counts as life/personhood;
- whether regional consent matters during existential crisis;
- how much uncertainty is tolerable.

# Primary Custodian characters

## Executor Rhen Marr — Meridian Custodian

Field commander/operator for Crown alignment.

Traits:

- technically brilliant;
- controlled;
- not sadistic;
- willing to override regional choices;
- genuinely fears habitat loss.

Function:

- main boss;
- clearest Restoration argument.

## Director Sera Vonn — Assembly Coordinator

Non-combat senior representative.

Function:

- presents orbital-state evidence;
- negotiates before/after confrontation;
- may become late-game support/obstruction depending events.

## Jalen Orr — Crown Systems Officer

Mid-level operator skeptical of forced alignment.

Function:

- shows internal Custodian dissent;
- can provide alternate route or evidence if optional interactions completed.

## Pera Linn — Safety Auditor

Function:

- documents actual failures caused by both historical systems and Wayfarer reconfiguration;
- prevents narrative from cherry-picking evidence.

## Captain Tovin Ashe — Security Lead

Function:

- practical enforcement voice;
- less ideological;
- focused on keeping Crown from collapsing.

# Regional voices returning

At least 3 prior-region characters or representatives should appear/communicate in the Crown.

Selection depends on state.

Their role:

- report concrete consequences;
- disagree with each other;
- make accumulated choices feel real.

Avoid a simplistic “everyone you helped arrives to cheer.”

# Major narrative beats

## Crown Refuge

Workers explain Custodian occupation.

## Compliance Hall

Player receives strong evidence for the Restoration case.

## Assembly Forum

Direct debate over Periapsis.

No final choice.

## Meridian Custodian

Conflict becomes physical over control of the route.

## State Concourse

Player sees accumulated world state.

## Periapsis Gate

The question changes from “who controls the route?” to “what configuration should the Meridian become?”

# Tessel integration

Tessel should be present according to Choir outcome.

## Carrier Lock

- little direct expression;
- occasional constrained signals;
- Custodians argue this proves containment works.

## Open Chorus

- visible field communication;
- some Custodians treat this as evidence of uncontrolled spread;
- Tessel may assist or observe.

## Partitioned Accord

- bounded channels;
- evidence that negotiated coexistence can work in some sectors but not perfectly.

Do not let Tessel simply explain the correct answer.

# Art direction

## Recognition layer

Reuse Transit motifs:

- rails;
- clamp housings;
- Relay pylons;
- old route signs;
- maintenance catwalks;
- service doors;
- training structures.

## Catastrophe layer

Add:

- rotated rooms;
- torn hull;
- exterior exposure;
- emergency braces;
- broken rail arcs;
- regional materials;
- debris.

## Custodian layer

Custodian additions should look:

- temporary but precise;
- modular;
- standardized;
- high-visibility;
- field-safe.

Assets:

- suppression pylons;
- temporary anchor projectors;
- compliance barriers;
- control consoles;
- portable Relay bridges;
- emergency habitat seals.

Avoid imperial palace aesthetics.

# Visual contrast

Old Transit:

- worn;
- familiar;
- utilitarian.

Custodian overlay:

- clean;
- geometric;
- standardized;
- temporary.

Tessel/Phase:

- relational;
- incomplete;
- interference-based.

The three languages should coexist without visual confusion.

# Landmark

**The Broken Crown**

Upper Transit ring visibly split into several arcs around the route to Periapsis.

From several rooms, players see:

- detached ring segments;
- gas giant;
- Anchor lines;
- Cloud Loom;
- Periapsis structure.

# Audio

Custodian sound language:

- precise;
- short;
- procedural;
- less organic than regional machinery.

Key cues:

- suppression lock;
- anchor deployment;
- seizure warning;
- compliance gate;
- emergency authority.

Crown ambience:

- broken rail groans;
- exterior wind/current;
- old Relay chimes;
- distant structural impacts.

# Music

The Crown score should quote/recontextualize early Transit motifs.

Goals:

- recognition;
- loss;
- escalation;
- institutional tension.

Custodian sections add:

- strict meter;
- clipped mechanical rhythm.

Tessel/state variations may alter harmonic layer.

Avoid generic final-fortress music.

# Accessibility

Custodian warnings require:

- shape;
- icon;
- animation;
- optional audio.

No critical order/authority cue is audio-only.

# Production constraints

- transformed Transit should reuse/modularly modify early assets where believable;
- state variants use component swaps;
- Custodian kit must work across many rooms;
- prior-region materials should be limited to readable accents;
- no variant may multiply environment budget uncontrollably.

# Acceptance criteria

Players should understand:

- what Custodians want;
- why their position is rational;
- what their plan would overwrite;
- why the Wayfarers resist;
- that internal Custodian disagreement exists;
- how prior regional choices changed the argument;
- that Periapsis remains a genuine decision after the boss.
