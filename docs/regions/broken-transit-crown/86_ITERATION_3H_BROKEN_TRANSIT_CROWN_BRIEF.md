# Iteration 3H — Broken Transit Crown Production Brief

**Status:** Production-specification pass  
**Scope:** Broken Transit Crown topology, transformed Transit grammar, Custodian occupation, Meridian Custodian, Anchor Knight, Astral Shepherd optional major encounter, prior-world-state aggregation, assets, networking/performance, economy, sequence-break safety and Periapsis entry  
**Version 1 assumptions:** 2.5D, PC-first, solo + 1–4 online co-op, Unreal Engine 5.8.x, host-authoritative listen server

## Purpose

Broken Transit Crown is the first major **mastery/convergence region**.

It does not teach a new foundational movement verb.

Its job is to prove that the complete Resonance toolkit can be recombined under pressure while the player revisits architecture they remember from the opening hours in a catastrophically changed state.

Core promise:

> **The world you learned has become the final exam.**

The region must feel recognizably Transit Spine while showing orbital detachment, rotated infrastructure, Anchor outcome consequences, Custodian occupation, Tessel/Phase anomalies, altered currents, dead/reactivated machinery and failed Relay lines.

## Canon carried forward

Foundational toolkit before Crown:

- Attract;
- Repel;
- Link / Echo;
- Latch;
- Orbit;
- Vector Shift;
- Flux;
- Phase;
- Invert.

No new foundational traversal ability is introduced here.

Overdrive remains optional mastery.

The gas-giant orbital habitat is locked.

Tessel is real as distributed field intelligence, while identity/origin remain unresolved.

## Pacing decision — Astral Shepherd

**Astral Shepherd is not mandatory on the critical Crown route.**

It becomes an optional major mastery encounter reached from the Outer Alignment Ring.

Reasons:

- Crown already culminates in Meridian Custodian;
- two mandatory major bosses before Periapsis compress pacing;
- the human Custodian confrontation needs narrative focus;
- Shepherd works better as a high-skill systems boss that rewards completionists/co-op groups.

Defeating Shepherd provides:

- alternate Crown route;
- Periapsis stabilization advantage;
- high-tier reward;
- additional Anchor Zero context.

It does not gate the finale.

## Regional identity

Broken Transit Crown has no new single gimmick.

Its identity comes from **system collision**:

- Transit rails detached into moving anchors;
- old gravity machinery partially restored;
- Flux-inert maintenance structures;
- Phase-overlapped Relay remnants;
- Cloud Loom currents crossing old transit corridors;
- Custodian anti-Resonance devices;
- regional-state variants layered onto familiar spaces.

## Memory design rule

At least 40–50% of critical-route spaces should visibly reuse or echo a recognizable early Transit landmark, route, service room, Relay or machine family.

The player should repeatedly think:

> “I remember this place.”

Then discover that the old solution no longer works.

Reuse must be intentional narrative geography, not asset repetition disguised as content.

## Region structure

### Act A — Return to the Spine

Re-enter transformed Transit.

Goals:

- recognize old landmarks;
- demonstrate current world-state aggregation;
- encounter first Custodian patrol;
- learn anti-Resonance counterplay.

### Act B — Crown Fracture

Transit architecture becomes increasingly detached/rotated.

Goals:

- full-toolkit rooms;
- Anchor Knight;
- mixed Phase/Flux/current routes;
- Custodian route denial.

### Act C — Assembly Occupation

Enter Custodian-controlled upper Crown.

Goals:

- understand Custodian argument;
- meet human operators;
- challenge their restoration plan;
- optional Astral Shepherd branch.

### Act D — Meridian Custodian

Fight the Custodian operator connected to infrastructure-scale control.

After victory/resolution:

- Crown route to Periapsis opens;
- world-state summary becomes visible through infrastructure;
- faction/Tessel positions sharpen.

## Critical-path duration

- critical route: 3–4 hours;
- exploratory including Shepherd/optional mastery: 5–7 hours.

This is intentionally dense rather than geographically enormous.

## Mastery rule

Every critical room may assume competence with **several** known verbs, but should not require every verb at once.

Typical room budget:

- 2–3 primary verbs;
- 1 optional optimization verb;
- 1 co-op/Echo responsibility.

Boss/setpiece rooms may use more.

This limits cognitive overload.

## Custodian combat philosophy

Custodians use the same engineering language as the player, but with different priorities.

They can:

- deploy temporary anchors;
- lock or suppress tagged anchors;
- seize or interrupt Link;
- stabilize against force;
- reorient platforms;
- mirror/invert selected relationships;
- Phase selected units;
- Flux protected infrastructure.

They do **not** simply disable the player's abilities wholesale.

Counterplay must remain visible and reversible.

## Adaptive counterplay rule

Custodian enemies may react to repeated patterns, but they do not read player inputs unfairly.

They observe authored gameplay events such as:

- same anchor used repeatedly;
- repeated projectile reflection;
- repeated aerial route;
- excessive Link dependence.

Response is:

- telegraphed;
- temporary;
- specific;
- avoidable.

## Prior-world-state aggregation

Crown uses derived state groups rather than bespoke combinations.

Required aggregate groups:

- Power Reserve;
- Water/Thermal Stability;
- Structural Access;
- Field Communication;
- Atmospheric Support;
- Habitat/Adaptation Support.

These affect:

- current strength;
- powered machinery;
- Phase visibility;
- available support NPCs;
- hazards;
- shortcuts;
- enemy composition.

No combination may soft-lock the critical path.

## Co-op design

### Solo

Echo supplies required simultaneous actor.

### 2P

Direct paired responsibilities.

### 3P

Pair + flex/rescue/control.

### 4P

Two active pairs or multiple subsystems.

Late-game rooms must avoid:

- four players stacked on one anchor;
- one player permanently holding a switch;
- waiting for host to solve state.

## Narrative function

Custodian Assembly becomes a real political/technical faction.

Their position:

- Meridian orbital failure is imminent;
- validated Anchor configurations have known models;
- unverified Wayfarer changes increased uncertainty;
- Tessel communication can propagate uncontrolled;
- central stabilization may require overriding regional adaptations.

Their case must contain real evidence.

Their methods also create real costs.

The game should not collapse the conflict into “authoritarian bad guys versus freedom.”

## Quality gates

- players recognize early-game geography;
- no new foundational ability is needed;
- all major abilities receive meaningful use across the region;
- Custodian counters feel intelligent, not ability-denial cheating;
- four-player rooms distribute responsibilities;
- every world-state combination preserves a critical path;
- Astral Shepherd feels valuable but optional;
- Meridian Custodian remains the emotional/narrative climax;
- region ends with clear Periapsis stakes.

## Exit criteria

Iteration 3H is complete when:

- Crown is greybox-ready;
- transformed topology is coherent;
- Custodian enemy grammar is production-defined;
- Anchor Knight, Astral Shepherd and Meridian Custodian are fully specified;
- prior-world-state aggregation maps to room variants;
- late-game loadout/economy rules are reviewed;
- sequence-break/soft-lock rules are specified;
- Periapsis entry can derive safely from saved world state;
- network/performance risks are identified with pass/fail spikes.
