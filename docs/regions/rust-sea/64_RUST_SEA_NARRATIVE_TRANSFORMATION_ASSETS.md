# Rust Sea — Narrative, Transformation, Art, Audio and Asset Requirements

**Purpose:** Give Rust Sea a lived culture and thematic identity around salvage, buried infrastructure, emergent ecology and the politics of excavation.

# Narrative function

Gravity Orchard asks whether adaptation deserves preservation.

Solar Foundry asks how recovered power should be distributed.

Flooded Observatory asks who owns a resource transformed by catastrophe.

Rust Sea asks:

> **When a ruined city becomes the foundation of a new economy and ecology, is excavation recovery—or another displacement?**

The central regional conflict is not “old city good, dust bad.”

Both states support real lives.

# Rust Sea communities

The Rust Sea is inhabited by tower settlements, salvage cooperatives, route-readers and machine-city archivists.

People survive by understanding particulate movement.

Cultural practices:

- tide/front forecasting;
- marking buried structures by remembered coordinates;
- anchoring homes to tower crowns;
- salvage rights negotiated by exposure cycle;
- inheritance of route knowledge;
- festivals/rituals around rare city exposures;
- strong taboo against activating unknown buried systems without warning neighboring settlements.

The culture should feel technically competent, not primitive scavenger stereotype.

# Regional quest structure

## Main quest

1. reach Breakwater Camp;
2. learn buried city / Sea conflict;
3. discover inert lattice limitation;
4. acquire Flux Field;
5. use Flux in traversal/combat/mechanisms;
6. enter Buried City;
7. defeat Undertow Engine;
8. map Cathedral control knots;
9. confront Dust Cathedral;
10. choose basin circulation.

## Optional — Buried Names

Recover location records of old machine-city districts.

Reveals:

- some buried structures remain valuable;
- others were deliberately abandoned before the fracture;
- City Excavation is not automatically equivalent to restoring homes.

## Optional — Living Current

Document particulate-dependent organisms/machine colonies.

Reveals:

- which Sea systems are emergent ecology;
- which are simply dangerous autonomous processes;
- what Stable Tides preserves.

## Optional — Migration Math

Investigate where a Directed Migration could go.

Possible destinations depend on prior state.

Examples:

- unused Transit containment sector;
- Foundry industrial sink;
- selected shield/ballast zone;
- area where ferric mass could stabilize or instead endanger infrastructure.

Unlocks Directed Migration only when a viable route exists.

## Optional — Inert by Design

Explore machine-city archive showing why old lattice had low Resonant response.

Narrative value:

Flux becomes morally/technically complicated.

The player is learning to override an old safety boundary.

# Regional decision

## A — City Excavation

Shift the Sea away from major historical city sectors.

### Local benefits

- towers/bridges exposed;
- fabrication and transport infrastructure recovered;
- new conventional routes;
- archival access.

### Local costs

- current salvage platforms become stranded/unsafe;
- particulate-dependent ecology migrates or declines;
- displaced Sea buries other areas;
- some dormant machines reactivate.

### External

Potential:

- manufacturing/resources improve;
- old city systems draw more Foundry power.

## B — Stable Tides

Maintain a controlled dynamic particulate Sea.

### Local benefits

- existing tower/salvage culture remains;
- dynamic traversal persists;
- ecological/current systems survive;
- lower relocation shock.

### Local costs

- much machine-city infrastructure remains buried;
- maintenance remains specialized and risky;
- some old services never return.

### External

Less recovered industrial capacity.

Potentially fewer dormant hazards activated.

## C — Directed Migration

Move a large particulate mass to a selected external basin/containment route.

### Local benefits

- selected city sector exposed;
- dangerous Cathedral-adjacent accumulation reduced.

### Local costs

- Sea culture/resource base shrinks;
- one or more local routes disappear;
- some ecological systems collapse/relocate.

### External

Destination receives both benefit and hazard.

Examples:

- ballast/shielding mass;
- industrial feedstock;
- buried machinery interference;
- new Ferric enemy variants.

Choice text must name the actual destination and known consequences.

# Prior world-state interactions

## Foundry

### Historical Grid

- more buried machinery is powered on arrival;
- City Excavation has higher immediate industrial value and higher threat activation.

### Civic

- fewer dormant machines;
- local camp/Relay support stronger.

### Spectrum

- Flux behavior around selected lattice exhibits anomalous Resonant patterns, foreshadowing Choir.

## Observatory

### Historic Channel / Relief

Water exposure creates oxidized/compacted Sea zones.

One buried district may be easier to excavate but some mechanisms degraded.

### Suspended Basin

Rust Sea remains drier/looser in linked route.

Different front geometry.

# Transformation implementation

## City Excavation afterstate

Visual:

- broad Sea recession;
- exposed city streets/towers;
- new hard surfaces.

NPC:

- archivists move downward;
- some salvagers protest/relocate;
- workshops expand.

Enemies:

- more old-machine threats;
- fewer Dredger ambush zones.

## Stable Tides afterstate

Visual:

- fronts become more regular/predictable;
- tower settlement routes remain.

NPC:

- route culture expands;
- archivists retain smaller excavation program.

Enemies:

- particulate families remain prominent.

## Directed Migration afterstate

Visual:

- a huge outbound particulate corridor/front;
- selected city sector exposed;
- local Sea visibly diminished.

NPC:

- logistics/migration teams appear;
- some salvage businesses close or move.

External destination gets new state.

# Art kit

## Architecture

- buried machine-city walls;
- bridge ribs;
- tower crowns;
- field-control pylons;
- salvage platforms;
- ferric breakwaters;
- old conduits;
- conditioning stations;
- half-exposed transit pieces.

## Particulate presentation

Need scalable layers:

- distant density field;
- mid-range GPU particles;
- local contact particles;
- front crest;
- wake;
- excavation lift-away;
- compacted surface material.

Gameplay collision does not come from particles.

## Flux material language

Need consistent across all regions:

- inert lattice;
- priming;
- Fluxed;
- decaying.

Rust Sea is where the language is introduced, but it must work on old-region assets.

## Landmark

- shape subtly differs after certain encounters/material states.

- low field hum;

# Music

Palette:

- dry metallic percussion;
- bowed/processed metal;
- low machine drones;
- irregular granular rhythms;
- occasional human acoustic motif from tower settlements.

As player nears Cathedral, subtle sustained harmonic structure hints at Choir Array.

# Dialogue density

Critical route:

- roughly 3–5 minutes mandatory before Dust Cathedral if optional conversations skipped.

Optional social/archival material can be deeper.

# Co-op narrative

- active Wayfarers recognized;
- guests vote on basin outcome;
- no critical clue depends on banter;
- Flux acquisition scene works for 1–4 active players.

# Production constraints

- dense Sea visuals must scale;
- no per-particle gameplay collision;
- transformations reuse region geometry/state variants;
- city-exposed variants budgeted early;
- Flux VFX must be legible under four players;
- background storms cannot obscure hazards.

# Acceptance criteria

Players should understand:

- why people live on/in the current Rust Sea;
- what the buried city could provide;
- why inert lattice existed;
- what Flux overrides;
- what each regional outcome exposes, preserves or exports;
- how prior Foundry/Observatory decisions changed the region.
