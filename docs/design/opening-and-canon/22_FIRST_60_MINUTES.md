# First 60 Minutes — Beat-by-Beat Opening

## Goals of the opening hour

By approximately 60 minutes, a typical player should understand:

- the game is about movement and force, not conventional swordplay;
- Attract and Repel are universal verbs;
- the world is physically broken and can change;
- solo Echo and multiplayer cooperation solve the same multi-actor language differently;
- the four Wayfarers are characters with relationships;
- the Meridian's official restoration mission may be morally complicated;
- the player has seen one memorable boss-scale encounter and one visible world transformation.

The hour must work in solo and 2/3/4-player sessions.

# 0:00–0:05 — Cold open: Falling Meridian

## Scene

No long exposition.

The game opens with a Wayfarer transport crossing the exterior of the Meridian. In the background, enormous ring segments rotate at visibly inconsistent rates around a gas giant.

A Mass Anchor fails.

A whole section of transit infrastructure shears sideways. Gravity changes. The transport is torn apart.

Control begins during the failure.

## Player action

- basic movement;
- jump;
- automatic safe ledge recovery once;
- sprint across collapsing interior;
- one simple attack against a maintenance drone blocking an exit.

The player sees other named Wayfarers separated into adjacent routes if solo; in multiplayer, party members occupy nearby lanes.

## Narrative information

Only essentials:

- “Anchor Seven just dropped.”
- “The Transit Spine is losing alignment.”
- “Get to a field node.”

# 0:05–0:12 — Learn Attract

Player enters a damaged cargo spine with fixed Resonance nodes.

Sequence:

1. normal gap;
2. impossible gap with obvious field node;
3. prompt: **Hold Attract**;
4. player accelerates toward node;
5. later target is above and behind, teaching directional selection;
6. optional side ledge contains first small lore/salvage reward.

In multiplayer, players can see one another performing the action. They cannot yet Link deliberately.

# 0:12–0:18 — Learn Repel

The route ends in a collapsed shaft.

A field pulse pushes debris outward. Player learns:

- Repel from wall node;
- Repel light object;
- Repel hostile projectile back at a drone.

First “aha” moment: the same input moves the player, an object and a projectile depending on mass/context.

# 0:18–0:24 — First real combat

Encounter: 2–4 enemies depending on player count.

Enemy roles:

- Scrapper drone: melee pressure;
- Survey spark: light ranged projectile;
- loose armor plate on one enemy.

Teach:

- light attack;
- evade;
- crack armor;
- Attract loosened plate;
- Repel plate as projectile.

Fracture Health is introduced via a controlled low-damage hit scenario only if natural play has not shown it.

# 0:24–0:30 — Reunion Node / Echo or Link

Players reach a damaged Relay chamber.

## Multiplayer

The rig discovers synchronized Wayfarer signatures and unlocks **Resonance Link**.

Safe sandbox:

- Link teammate;
- move apart;
- simple pull;
- teammate acts as anchor;
- optional launch pad encourages experimentation.

## Solo

The Relay cannot form a live Link, so it reconstructs a temporal field trace: **Echo**.

Safe sandbox:

- record 3–4 seconds;
- replay self crossing a pressure node;
- player walks through door while Echo holds state.

The fiction therefore introduces Echo as the solo equivalent of synchronized field presence rather than as a random supernatural power.

# 0:30–0:36 — First cooperative traversal test

Room contains two sides of a broken rotating bridge.

## 2–4 players

At least two actors apply opposing force while others traverse or stabilize. With three/four players, extra players can shorten the sequence by holding a side route or catching debris, but are not idle.

## Solo

Record Echo maintaining one field direction; player performs the opposite action.

Success rotates a bridge section and physically reconnects the route behind them.

This is the first miniature version of the world-transformation promise.

# 0:36–0:42 — Character and story breath

The group reaches **Wayfarer Scar**, an emergency maintenance enclave built into wrecked transit structures.

Meet:

- Keph, traveling salvager;
- local Anchor technician;
- one survivor whose family route was cut off.

Player gets:

- first Relay save;
- map;
- Core/loadout explanation kept very small;
- first module choice from two side-grades.

Dialogue is optional after critical lines.

The Anchor technician says restoring the nearby stabilizer should “put the section back where it belongs.” Ily (if present) reacts to the phrase.

# 0:42–0:50 — Exploration fork

Two short routes open around the settlement.

Route A: movement-heavy exterior.

Route B: combat-heavy maintenance interior.

Both reconnect at the same objective. This teaches that the world supports route preference and early optional exploration.

Each route contains:

- one hidden pickup;
- one map blocker the player cannot yet solve;
- one sightline toward the Gravity Orchard, establishing the next region.

# 0:50–0:57 — Mini-boss: The Alignment Hound

A quadrupedal maintenance machine designed to clamp misaligned rail segments.

## Mechanics

Phase 1:

- charges along rail;
- foreleg armor can be cracked;
- player removes clamp plate and throws it back.

Phase 2:

- machine attaches itself between two rotating structures;
- players/Echo must create opposing force on exposed clamps;
- successful tear destabilizes it.

Player-count scaling:

- solo: one active clamp + Echo timing;
- 2 players: two simultaneous clamps;
- 3 players: two clamps plus roaming projectile node;
- 4 players: four short-lived clamp points in paired timing windows, not 4x health.

# 0:57–1:00 — The first consequence

Defeating the Hound restores access to the local stabilizer.

The player activates it.

A huge exterior ring segment rotates into alignment.

The settlement celebrates—briefly.

Then the rotation causes a distant floating habitat to lose the low-gravity condition sustaining its suspended farms. Through the window, the player sees water and vegetation begin falling into a new orientation.

Ily or another NPC says some version of:

> “We fixed our side.”

Beat.

> “We changed theirs.”

Title appears:

# RESONANCE

The Transit Spine opens and the Gravity Orchard becomes the first major destination.

# Player-count handling in the opening

## Solo

- Echo unlocked by minute ~25.
- NPC party members do not fight as AI companions; they remain narratively nearby/off-route.
- Multi-actor mechanics use Echo.

## Two players

- Both have meaningful tasks in Link tutorial and mini-boss.
- No section requires waiting for absent players 3/4.

## Three players

- Third player gets parallel stabilization/projectile responsibilities.
- Puzzle geometry contains optional acceleration route so third player is active.

## Four players

- Full four-player visual stress begins by the first combat.
- Mini-boss includes paired simultaneous tasks.
- VFX density is deliberately representative of target production constraints.

# First-hour rewards

By 60 minutes, player has:

- map access;
- Relay access;
- basic attacks;
- Attract;
- Repel;
- Link (multiplayer) / Echo (solo);
- first Module;
- first Core introduction;
- one mini-boss win;
- at least one unresolved map gate;
- a visible next destination;
- first proof that “repair” changes other communities.
