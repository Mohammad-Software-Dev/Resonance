# Core Game Loop

## Moment-to-moment loop

The smallest repeatable loop is:

1. **Read** the room: threats, anchors, materials, exits, hazards.
2. **Move** using running, jumping, evasion and Resonance.
3. **Manipulate** an object, enemy, teammate or environmental system.
4. **Commit** to an action: launch, attack, pull, redirect or phase.
5. **React** to the consequence.
6. **Recover or chain** into the next action.

This loop should happen every few seconds in active spaces.

## Exploration loop

1. Enter an unfamiliar space.
2. Notice a landmark, blocked route, unusual material or sound cue.
3. Traverse and fight through local challenges.
4. Find a shortcut, secret, NPC, module, lore fragment or progression clue.
5. Mark unresolved opportunities automatically or manually on the map.
6. Obtain an ability or trigger a world-state change.
7. Recognize new possibilities in previously visited regions.
8. Return through changed geometry/encounters and discover new content.

### Exploration reward hierarchy

The best reward is a new possibility, not a number.

Priority order:

1. Major ability / new system interaction.
2. New route / world connection.
3. Core or Module enabling a new build behavior.
4. Significant lore or character consequence.
5. Utility upgrade (health capacity, Resonance capacity, map function).
6. Currency/resource.

Currency alone should rarely sit at the end of the hardest secret path.

## Combat loop

Combat is based on positioning, momentum and material interaction.

1. Identify enemy properties: mass, armor, Resonance polarity, exposed components.
2. Create favorable position with movement.
3. Damage or destabilize a target.
4. Use Resonance to change target state: pull, throw, strip, suspend, redirect.
5. Convert the state change into damage, crowd control or mobility.
6. Use aggression to recover Fractured Health where appropriate.
7. Exit or chain to the next threat.

Co-op adds passing, pinning, opposing forces and simultaneous actions.

## Co-op social loop

The game should repeatedly generate short conversations like:

- “Stand there.”
- “Pull now.”
- “Throw it to me.”
- “I’ll hold this side.”
- “Go, I’ll catch you.”
- “Wait—there’s something below us.”

A good room lets players form and test plans without requiring lengthy explanation.

## Session loop (30–90 minutes)

A typical session should deliver several of the following:

- explore a new subregion;
- complete a character/settlement objective;
- unlock a shortcut;
- discover one meaningful secret;
- find one build component;
- fight a mini-boss or major boss;
- trigger a visible world transformation;
- learn or refine a cooperative technique.

Save points/Relays should align with likely session boundaries without forcing players to stop there.

## Campaign loop

The main campaign revolves around six damaged **Mass Anchors** and two endgame systems.

Each Anchor arc contains:

1. Arrival in a region affected by a distinctive physical failure.
2. Contact with inhabitants/faction conflict.
3. Discovery of a local traversal/combat expression of Resonance.
4. Branching exploration toward the Anchor.
5. One or more optional sub-objectives that affect the region.
6. Major Guardian encounter.
7. Anchor restoration/reconfiguration decision.
8. World-scale physical change.
9. New routes into at least two old/new regions.

The player does not need to repair all Anchors in one strict order after the opening arc. Mid-game should offer 2–3 viable objectives simultaneously.

## Critical path structure

### Act I: Orientation (roughly 0–4 hours)

- Learn basic movement, Attract and Repel.
- Reach first settlement.
- Discover that the Meridian is physically unstable.
- Restore first minor Anchor.
- Introduce Echo in solo or Resonance Link in co-op.
- Open the central transit spine.

### Act II: Expansion (roughly 4–14 hours)

- Multiple regional objectives open.
- Unlock Link, Orbit, Phase/Flux variants.
- Factions reveal incompatible ideas of “repair.”
- World changes begin altering earlier regions.
- Player builds become meaningful.

### Act III: Consequence (roughly 14–20 hours)

- The source of the Meridian's new emergent life becomes clear.
- Anchor decisions begin closing some options while opening others.
- Endgame regions require mastery rather than simply ownership of powers.
- Final route depends on accumulated world state.

### Postgame / completion

Optional superbosses, hidden Convergence Chambers, settlement conclusions, advanced traversal trials and alternate endings.

## Pacing rules

- Introduce at most one major new verb in a short learning window.
- Follow a new verb with safe experimentation before lethal testing.
- After a difficult boss, provide exploration, story or expressive movement before another intensity spike.
- Avoid more than ~20–25 minutes of uninterrupted mandatory combat.
- Avoid more than ~10 minutes of slow interaction/dialogue without optional movement.
- Every major region should contain one immediately recognizable landmark.
- Every 10–15 minutes on the critical path should offer a discovery, decision, spectacle, mechanical insight or character beat.

## Failure and retry loop

Normal exploration death:

1. Player destabilizes.
2. Co-op: brief rescue window if another player is nearby.
3. Respawn at last Relay or designated local recovery point.
4. Keep permanent progression and standard currency.
5. Reset most enemies and temporary combat state.

Boss failure:

- Respawn very near the arena.
- Boss intro can be skipped after first viewing.
- Loadout adjustment available before re-entry.
- Optional hint system activates only after repeated failures and must be dismissible.

## Completion metrics (design targets)

A healthy campaign should yield approximately:

- 18–22 hours typical first ending.
- 30–40 hours completionist.
- 60–70% map seen on an ordinary critical-path run.
- 80–90% on an exploratory first run.
- meaningful optional content remaining after credits.

These are targets, not promises; playtest completion-time distributions determine final scope.
