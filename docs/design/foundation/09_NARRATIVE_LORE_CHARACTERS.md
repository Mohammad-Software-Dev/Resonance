# Narrative, Lore and Characters

## Narrative goal

**Narrative density target:** moderate and character-driven. The campaign has a real central story, memorable named characters, regional quest arcs and consequential choices, while leaving substantial room for environmental storytelling and uninterrupted exploration.

Tell a story about **repair, inheritance and whether returning something to its original state is always desirable**.

The emotional core should be understandable without reading every lore object. Deeper history is optional and discoverable.

## Premise

The Meridian was created to sustain civilization in an extreme orbital environment. Its Mass Anchors controlled gravity, structure and energy distribution.

Centuries ago, the system fractured. Conventional history says the failure was a catastrophe.

The player eventually learns that the “failure” also created conditions for new societies and forms of intelligence—including distributed life inside Resonance fields—to emerge.

Now the Meridian's orbit is genuinely destabilizing. Doing nothing is not viable. But restoring the original configuration may erase what the broken world became.

## Player character framework

Players are **Wayfarers**, salvage-engineers trained to operate Resonance rigs.

The playable cast is **a named ensemble of Wayfarers with distinct personalities, relationships and visual identities**. Players can customize cosmetic rig elements, but the characters themselves are authored rather than blank avatars. This supports co-op banter, regional story perspectives and a stronger marketing identity while all characters retain the common baseline movement/Resonance kit.

All characters share baseline abilities; differences should be narrative/personality/cosmetic rather than locking movement powers.

## Proposed core cast

Names are placeholders until naming/style pass.

### Mara Venn — Field Engineer

Practical, curious, distrustful of institutional certainty. Natural tutorial perspective without being naive.

### Ilyon “Ily” Sare — Cartographic Ecologist

Treats changed ecosystems as legitimate life rather than damage. Provides strong opposition to simple restoration.

### Brann Oris — Former Anchor Custodian

Believes preservation requires restoring the system despite cost. Not a villain; has witnessed settlements die from continued instability.

### Tessel — Emergent Field Intelligence

Distributed consciousness communicating through the Choir Array and other Resonant systems. It may be one being, many beings or an interface humans incorrectly treat as singular.

### Keph — Transit Salvager / merchant-adjacent recurring NPC

Warm, opportunistic, travels as routes reopen. Gives the world humor without becoming a clone of a recognizable genre merchant archetype.

### The Custodian Assembly

A faction of descendants/inheritors of original maintenance doctrine. They preserve procedures that may no longer fit current reality.

## Factions

### Restorationists

Goal: restore Meridian as closely as possible to original configuration.

Strength: stability, saved lives, functional infrastructure.

Cost: ecological/cultural erasure.

### Adaptationists

Goal: stabilize orbit while preserving new world states.

Strength: respect emergent life and cultures.

Cost: technically risky; may leave permanent instability.

### Resonant Communion

People who intentionally integrate consciousness with the field network.

Goal: accelerate transformation into a post-physical civilization.

Strength: may solve resource constraints.

Cost: threatens bodily autonomy and conventional societies.

No faction should be purely good/evil.

## Main narrative structure

### Act I — Repair mandate

Wayfarers respond to worsening orbital instability. First Anchor repair appears unquestionably beneficial.

### Act II — Contradictions

Regional societies reveal that “broken” systems became foundations for life. The player discovers records suggesting the historic fracture may have involved deliberate intervention rather than accident.

### Act III — Origin of the fracture

Possible direction: the original system's central intelligence predicted ecological collapse or authoritarian control and distributed itself through the Anchor network, intentionally reducing central control. The exact truth should avoid a simple “evil AI” reveal.

### Act IV — Final configuration

Players choose how to stabilize the Meridian, influenced by Anchor states and faction relationships.

## Ending framework

### Restoration ending

Meridian returns to stable historical architecture. Many populations survive, infrastructure flourishes, but distributed/new ecological forms are diminished or lost.

### Adaptation ending

Orbit stabilizes through a new hybrid configuration. Regions remain physically strange; societies must cooperate. Highest technical risk but preserves diversity.

### Communion ending

Meridian transforms into a networked Resonant organism/civilization. Powerful but deeply changes human existence.

### Hidden reconciliation ending

Requires optional discoveries and carefully chosen regional states. May allow a more balanced outcome without being a consequence-free “golden ending.” A real tradeoff should remain.

## Story delivery

Use:

- environment;
- NPC movement after world changes;
- short conversations;
- regional rituals/work practices;
- machine logs sparingly;
- optional field recordings;
- boss behavior/context;
- architecture transitions.

Avoid excessive lore text as the only way to understand the central conflict.

## Co-op narrative handling

All active players appear in important gameplay scenes where technically feasible.

Dialogue should avoid pretending only one hero exists.

Optional contextual banter between named characters can occur during exploration, but:

- frequency is low;
- repeats are minimized;
- players can disable banter;
- critical clues never depend on hearing banter during combat.

Host owns world-state decisions by default, but votes can be shown. See networking/save document.

## Quest design

Quests should often involve consequences rather than errands.

Example:

A settlement relies on low gravity for agriculture. Restoring the local Anchor will destroy their current crops but reopen water systems for thousands elsewhere.

Options may include:

- restore immediately;
- find a hybrid calibration through optional exploration;
- delay and help relocate crop stock;
- side with another faction.

Results alter NPC locations, region visuals and future services.

## Themes

Primary:

- repair versus transformation;
- stewardship;
- inherited systems;
- cooperation across incompatible needs;
- identity in changing environments.

Secondary:

- friendship under pressure;
- expertise versus certainty;
- how infrastructure shapes culture;
- whether continuity of form equals continuity of life.

## Tone rules

- Mysterious does not mean incomprehensible.
- Melancholy does not mean every character is miserable.
- Humor comes from character/world interaction, not constant quips.
- Co-op banter can provide warmth while the setting remains serious.
- Avoid plague/infection-as-central-metaphor framing to keep thematic identity separate from common genre touchstones.
