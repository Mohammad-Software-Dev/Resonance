# Vision and Design Pillars

## Product vision

**Resonance** should become the action-Metroidvania people recommend when someone asks for a game that combines **precision exploration, expressive combat and meaningful co-op** without sacrificing solo play.

The intended fantasy is not “being a hero with a sword.” It is **becoming a master of force and relationship**: manipulating yourself, enemies, machines, the environment and other players through a small set of physically coherent powers.

## Player fantasy

The player should feel like an agile explorer-engineer who can turn the environment into a weapon and a route. Early play is about survival and understanding. Mid-game play is about intentionally chaining mechanics. Late-game play is about improvising with the world at speed.

In co-op, the fantasy expands: another player is not a follower or damage multiplier. They are a moving anchor, rescue line, projectile source, combo partner, puzzle actor and alternate viewpoint.

## Target audience

Primary audience:

- Players who enjoy Metroidvanias, action platformers and boss-focused games.
- Players who value precise controls, exploration, secrets and environmental storytelling.
- Friends or couples seeking a substantial co-op campaign rather than a party game.
- Solo players who enjoy systems that reward experimentation and mastery.

Secondary audience:

- Speedrunners and challenge players.
- Players who enjoy puzzle-platforming when puzzles emerge from movement systems.
- Streamers/creators looking for shareable “save,” “launch,” “combo” and boss moments.

The game should not depend on players enjoying loot treadmills, crafting spreadsheets or long dialogue sequences.

## Pillar 1 — Kinetic mastery

Movement is the primary pleasure. The basic character must feel good before upgrades exist.

Requirements:

- Immediate acceleration with limited animation-induced input delay.
- Reliable jump arcs and generous but controlled jump buffering/coyote time.
- Fast recovery after ordinary attacks.
- Resonance powers usable in traversal without opening radial menus.
- Advanced movement arises from combinations rather than hidden arbitrary techniques.
- High skill ceiling without requiring pixel-perfect inputs for normal completion.

A room with no enemies should still allow players to enjoy swinging, launching, redirecting and catching momentum.

## Pillar 2 — Systemic Resonance

Attraction and Repulsion are not colored locks. They are verbs that apply consistently.

A magnetic/Resonant object should obey the same core rules whether it is:

- scenery,
- a projectile,
- a shield,
- an enemy armor plate,
- a puzzle weight,
- a traversal anchor,
- or a boss component.

Later powers modify the same grammar instead of replacing it.

## Pillar 3 — Cooperation as a moveset

Co-op mechanics must create interdependence without creating helplessness.

Players can always run, jump, fight and explore independently. Cooperation becomes optimal or uniquely expressive in selected situations.

Desired cooperative moments:

- catch a falling friend;
- slingshot a friend to a distant ledge;
- pass a projectile or enemy between players;
- hold opposite forces on a boss component;
- create a moving bridge/link;
- split into parallel routes and reconverge;
- synchronize a timing sequence;
- improvise a rescue after a mistake.

Avoid designing the campaign as a sequence of “Player A stands on switch while Player B walks through door.”

## Pillar 4 — Solo parity through Echo

Solo mode receives a bespoke system, **Echo**, that records and replays short windows of player action.

Echo should create:

- timing puzzles,
- synchronized attacks,
- multi-position manipulation,
- self-assisted traversal,
- and high-skill boss strategies.

Echoes do not make independent tactical decisions. They repeat recorded actions. This keeps solo play expressive and predictable rather than introducing an unreliable AI companion.

Required campaign content must be designed so that either a human partner or Echo can satisfy multi-actor requirements.

## Pillar 5 — A world that changes because of you

The Meridian is a damaged orbital habitat made of rotating structures. Repairing Anchors physically changes the world.

Progress should cause:

- regions to rotate or reconnect;
- gravity vectors to change;
- water/particle flows to relocate;
- ecosystems to migrate;
- NPC travel routes to open;
- previously decorative machines to activate;
- old paths to become shortcuts or hazards;
- new enemies or friendly populations to appear.

Backtracking therefore becomes inspection of consequences, not merely checking doors.

## Pillar 6 — Challenge without punishment loops

The game can be difficult, but retries should be fast.

Rules:

- no mandatory corpse run;
- main boss checkpoints nearby;
- currency retained on normal death unless a specific optional challenge says otherwise;
- failures explain themselves visually;
- exploration deaths respawn quickly;
- teammate rescue opportunities create tension before death in co-op;
- difficulty is primarily mechanical and cognitive, not economic punishment.

## Pillar 7 — Mystery with legibility

The setting should invite interpretation, but objectives and mechanics should not be opaque by accident.

Story mysteries may remain ambiguous. Gameplay states should not.

Players should be able to answer:

- What can I interact with?
- Which force is currently active?
- Why did I take damage?
- What changed when the Anchor moved?
- What does my teammate need from me?

## Emotional arc

### Early game — Awe and instability

The player enters a beautiful but visibly broken world. Resonance feels dangerous and improvised.

### Mid game — Competence and connection

Movement becomes expressive. Settlements and factions become understandable. Co-op combinations begin feeling intentional.

### Late game — Responsibility

The player realizes “repairing” the Meridian is not morally neutral. Restoring the old system may destroy new forms of life that depend on the broken state.

### Finale — Choice and consequence

Final decisions modify both narrative outcomes and the physical state of the world.

## Non-goals

Resonance is not intended to be:

- a Soulslike economy built around recovering lost currency;
- a traditional RPG driven by level numbers;
- an MMO or persistent shared world;
- a procedural roguelike;
- a loot grinder;
- a crafting-survival game;
- a puzzle game where combat is incidental;
- a competitive PvP title;
- a direct imitation of another Metroidvania's visual or narrative identity.

## “Ten-second identity” test

A muted ten-second clip should ideally show at least one uniquely identifiable action:

- a player orbiting another player;
- two players pulling opposite sides of a boss apart;
- an Echo repeating a recorded traversal action;
- a friend catching another player mid-fall;
- an enemy armor plate ripped off and turned into a projectile;
- a whole section of world rotating and creating new geography.

If a clip could be mistaken for a generic 2D action-platformer with the UI removed, the scene is not expressing the game's core identity strongly enough.
