# Business, Marketing and Community Strategy

## Positioning

The marketing message should not be “our Hollow Knight.” That weakens originality and places the game in an unfavorable direct comparison.

Recommended external positioning:

> **A 1–4 player action-Metroidvania where attraction, repulsion and energy links turn your friends—or echoes of yourself—into part of your movement and combat system.**

## Product hooks

Primary:

1. **Your friends become part of your moveset.**
2. **Solo players cooperate with Echoes of their own recorded actions.**
3. **A world physically restructures as you repair it.**
4. **Fast, skillful movement/combat built from one force system.**

Secondary:

- systemic bosses;
- rescues and accidental comedy;
- co-op exploration/secrets;
- meaningful world-state choices.

## Trailer structure hypothesis

First 10 seconds:

- player launches from anchor;
- friend catches and slings them;
- both rip boss component apart.

Next 10 seconds:

- solo player records Echo;
- Echo repeats action while current player takes different route;
- transition to transformed world geography.

Next 10 seconds:

- four-player boss spectacle;
- rescue from fall;
- enemy armor thrown between players;
- title/tagline.

Do not spend the opening trailer on lore narration before showing the mechanic.

## Demo strategy

A public demo can be valuable because movement and co-op are experiential.

Ideal demo:

- 30–60 minutes;
- starts quickly;
- teaches Attract/Repel;
- supports solo Echo and 2-player online/local if stable;
- ends with a boss or major world transformation;
- contains one optional secret requiring advanced movement.

The demo should collect anonymous gameplay telemetry only with appropriate disclosure/consent where required.

## Viral/shareable moments

Design systems that naturally generate clips:

- last-second rescue;
- launch gone wrong;
- unexpected object ricochet;
- synchronized boss break;
- speedrunner orbit chain;
- Echo choreography;
- region rotation spectacle.

These should come from gameplay rather than scripted “content creator moments.”

## Community development

Before launch:

- regular short clips explaining one mechanic at a time;
- public devlogs focused on discoveries/iteration rather than promises;
- closed playtest community;
- speedrun-oriented test group once movement stabilizes;
- accessibility playtests;
- networking tests across regions/latencies.

Avoid announcing release timing before multiplayer scope is proven.

## Pricing hypothesis

Premium product.

Initial target range: **USD $24.99–29.99** depending on final length, production quality, platform scope and market conditions at launch.

No pay-to-win. No paid power modules. Avoid live-service obligations unless the game's post-launch community clearly justifies expansions.

## DLC / expansion philosophy

Potential post-launch expansion only if base game is successful:

- one new region;
- new Guardian(s);
- new Core or ability extension that does not fragment multiplayer compatibility;
- challenge Chambers;
- narrative continuation.

Free updates can include balance, accessibility and selected challenge content.

## Cosmetics

Base-game cosmetics earned through exploration/challenges are preferable.

If paid cosmetics are ever considered, they must not affect gameplay and should not compromise visual readability. Current recommendation: **do not design business model around cosmetic microtransactions**.

## Platform plan

Suggested business sequence:

1. PC prototype/vertical slice.
2. Validate controller and online architecture.
3. Evaluate Steam/PC market interest through demo/wishlists.
4. Begin console port/certification planning early enough to avoid architecture surprises.
5. Cross-play only if budget/platform agreements support it.

## Key marketing proof points

Do not claim features until demonstrated:

- 4-player support;
- cross-play;
- local + online hybrid;
- campaign length;
- release window.

## Competitive differentiation test

Before greenlight, show anonymous short gameplay clips with titles hidden. Ask viewers:

- What is the game about?
- What mechanic do you remember?
- What other games does it resemble?
- What seems unique?
- Would you rather play alone or with a friend, and why?

If the main memory is “looks like [specific famous Metroidvania],” revise identity.

## Commercial KPI hypotheses

Before full production, track:

- demo completion rate;
- boss retry/finish rate;
- proportion playing solo vs co-op;
- average co-op session duration;
- wishlist conversion from trailer/demo traffic;
- replay rate of traversal challenge;
- organic clip/share creation;
- survey measure: “I want to show/play this with a friend.”

Exact sales targets should be created only after budget/team schedule is known.
