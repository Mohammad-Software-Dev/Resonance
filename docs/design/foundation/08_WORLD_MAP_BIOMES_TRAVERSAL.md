# World, Map, Biomes and Traversal

## The Meridian

The Meridian is a colossal artificial habitat formed from multiple rotating rings and suspended structures orbiting a failing star/gas-giant system. Long ago, its Mass Anchors failed or were intentionally altered. The world fractured into mechanically and ecologically distinct regions.

The structure provides a natural reason for:

- impossible gravity;
- rotating geography;
- floating oceans;
- isolated cultures;
- ancient machinery;
- reconfigurable routes;
- region-scale transformations.

## Macro map philosophy

The map should feel like a place rather than a level-select graph.

Principles:

- regions interconnect in multiple ways;
- major shortcuts produce “I know where I am” moments;
- verticality and rotated geometry matter;
- early landmarks remain visible from later zones where possible;
- restored Anchors create new cross-region links;
- critical path is understandable without being linear.

## Proposed regions

### 1. Wayfarer Scar / Opening Wreck

Purpose: tutorial and inciting incident.

Visuals: shattered transit hulls, exposed starscape, emergency fields, drifting cargo.

Mechanics: basic Attract/Repel, first rescue/Echo demonstration.

Transformation: debris field stabilizes into a permanent route.

### 2. Gravity Orchard

A living agricultural cylinder whose gravity has fractured. Trees grow from walls and ceiling; seed pods orbit local anchor nodes.

Mechanics:

- orbit traversal;
- moving organic masses;
- wildlife using gravity seams;
- vertical orientation changes.

Settlement: horticultural society adapting to multi-directional growth.

### 3. Solar Foundry

Massive mirror arrays and glassworks once supplied energy to the Meridian.

Mechanics:

- heat timing;
- mirror orientation;
- molten glass hazards;
- solar pressure machinery;
- armor-heavy constructs.

Transformation: restored mirrors power dormant systems across other regions.

### 4. Flooded Observatory

Water escaped normal basins and now forms enormous free-floating spheres around research architecture.

Mechanics:

- entering/exiting water globes changes momentum;
- moving water masses through fields;
- visibility/refraction puzzles;
- aquatic predators.

Transformation: changing gravity causes water to fall into ancient channels, revealing buried spaces and flooding others.

### 5. Rust Sea

A “sea” of metallic dust and machine fragments behaving like granular fluid under Resonance fields.

Mechanics:

- reveal buried structures by pulling particles;
- surf shifting particulate waves;
- hidden anchors;
- creatures that camouflage by coating themselves.

Transformation: Anchor activation causes the sea to migrate and uncover an old city.

### 6. Choir Array

Gigantic communication spires that produce resonant tones when forces pass through them.

Mechanics:

- sound timing;
- frequency matching;
- Links transmitting energy/signal;
- enemies reacting to sonic fields.

Narrative: contact with distributed consciousness living in the Meridian's field network.

### 7. Cloud Loom

Atmospheric harvesting machines dipping into the planet/star's upper layers.

Mechanics:

- powerful wind/flow;
- moving industrial kites;
- gas pockets;
- long-distance launches;
- spectacular exterior traversal.

Transformation: activates weather/energy patterns visible across the world.

### 8. Periapsis Core

Endgame machinery controlling the Meridian's orbit and systemic state.

Mechanics combine previous abilities under high stakes. Geography itself changes during traversal.

## Hub philosophy

Avoid one all-purpose town that trivializes travel. Use several settlements with distinct functions and characters.

A central **Transit Spine** becomes an early connective hub but does not replace regional identity.

Services can migrate as routes reopen.

## Fast travel

Unlock relatively early after the first main Anchor arc.

System: **Relay Network**.

Rules:

- discovered Relays become destinations;
- travel from Relay to Relay initially;
- later upgrade permits return to nearest Relay from safe states;
- no travel during active combat;
- co-op requires group confirmation or allows independent arrival with regroup logic.

Fast travel should reduce dead time, not replace traversal discovery.

## World transformations

Every major Anchor should alter at least three things:

1. Macro route connectivity.
2. Local room geometry/hazard state.
3. NPC/ecology/encounter distribution.

Example: Flooded Observatory stabilization

Before:

- water spheres suspended throughout labs;
- upper corridors dry;
- aquatic creatures isolated.

After:

- water drains down rotated channels;
- lower city floods;
- upper telescopes become accessible;
- aquatic predators migrate;
- a merchant relocates by boat;
- old “empty” shafts become waterfalls used as traversal routes.

## Map presentation

Map should communicate structure without revealing every secret.

Features:

- rooms/major connections;
- Relay locations;
- custom pings/markers;
- teammate locations;
- automatically remembered blocked mechanisms once inspected;
- changed-world indicators after Anchor events;
- optional late-game completion overlay.

The map can note *why* a route was blocked (“field strength insufficient,” “phase barrier”) once the player has examined it. When the relevant power is acquired, the marker updates to “potentially accessible.”

## Secret philosophy

Secrets should vary in discovery method:

- suspicious geometry;
- sound cue;
- Resonance response;
- NPC clue;
- ecology clue;
- advanced movement route;
- co-op/Echo synchronization;
- world-state return.

Do not rely mainly on breakable-looking walls.

## Sequence flexibility

After the opening, players should have choices among several regions. However, the game is not fully open-world.

Use layered gating:

- hard ability gates;
- soft skill gates;
- environmental state gates;
- narrative/faction gates;
- optional sequence breaks for advanced players.

Intentional sequence breaks are desirable when they do not corrupt saves or skip necessary onboarding.
