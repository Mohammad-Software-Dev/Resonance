# Legal and Originality Guardrails

> This document is a design-risk checklist, not legal advice. Before commercial release, obtain qualified IP/trademark counsel in relevant markets.

## Design objective

Resonance may use genre-level ideas common to action-Metroidvanias—interconnected exploration, ability-gated progression, boss fights, platforming and responsive melee combat—while developing independent expression in characters, world, narrative, art, UI, sound, progression, encounters and implementation.

The creative goal should be stronger than “legally different enough.” It should be **recognizably its own game**.

## What to borrow only at the abstract level

Acceptable inspiration categories:

- responsive 2D movement;
- nonlinear/interconnected maps;
- gradual mobility expansion;
- challenging bosses;
- hidden areas;
- environmental storytelling;
- melancholic/wondrous tone;
- precision combat;
- meaningful backtracking.

These should be implemented with Resonance's own systems and fiction.

## Deliberate differentiation

### Protagonist

Use:

- human/human-derived adult Wayfarer;
- visible face or original facial treatment;
- asymmetrical field suit;
- kinetic gauntlet system.

Avoid converging on:

- small masked/horned silhouette;
- insectoid identity;
- tiny cloaked knight proportions;
- needle/nail-like signature weapons.

### World

Use:

- orbital megastructure;
- rotating rings;
- unstable gravity;
- industrial ecology;
- multiple living societies adapting to infrastructure.

Avoid making the primary setting a ruined subterranean insect kingdom, plague/infection kingdom or pilgrimage-to-citadel story.

### UI

Create original:

- health representation;
- map visual language;
- module/loadout screen;
- currency iconography;
- save/Relay presentation.

Avoid mask-like health icons, charm/notch-like visual metaphors, or close layout imitation.

### Save/checkpoint fiction

Use **Relays** / network stabilization nodes with original visual/function language.

Do not reproduce bench-centered presentation or corpse/shadow recovery structure.

### Progression

Use Cores, Modules and rig bandwidth with behavior-changing effects.

Ensure UI, naming, acquisition and tuning are independently developed.

### Combat

Use momentum, gauntlets, armor manipulation, Fracture Health and Resonance.

Avoid a basic weapon/energy/heal loop that visually and mechanically mirrors a specific reference title.

### Narrative

Core theme: repairing inherited infrastructure may erase adaptations/new life.

Avoid direct analogues of specific kingdoms, rulers, vessels, infection sources, dream realms, named archetypes or plot reveals from reference games.

## “Stacked similarity” test

Even if individual elements are generic, a combination can still feel derivative.

At each milestone ask:

- Does character silhouette evoke a specific existing character?
- Does screenshot composition resemble a specific game because of palette + UI + architecture + proportions simultaneously?
- Does a boss concept map too directly to a recognizable boss?
- Does an NPC combine role, design, mannerism and location in a familiar way?
- Does a progression system reproduce another game's terminology or visual metaphor?

If yes, change multiple layers, not just names.

## Reference process

Maintain an inspiration board from **many sources**, not one game.

Possible inputs:

- real orbital habitat concepts;
- industrial machinery;
- magnetic-fluid experiments;
- botanical adaptation research/imagery;
- kinetic sculpture;
- brutalist/modernist infrastructure;
- underwater observatories;
- diverse action-platformers/co-op games for interaction patterns.

Artists/designers should document source categories and create synthesis sketches rather than tracing specific game assets.

## Trademark/naming process

“Resonance” is a working title only.

Before public branding:

1. generate a shortlist of distinctive names;
2. search game storefronts and web usage;
3. search relevant trademark databases;
4. obtain counsel for clearance if budget permits;
5. secure domains/social handles after reasonable clearance.

Do not assume an available Steam title means trademark safety.

## Patent/technology review

Gameplay ideas are often broadly reusable, but specific patented systems can exist. Before shipping unusual network/interaction features, counsel can perform a targeted patent review if warranted.

## Asset provenance

Track source/license for:

- fonts;
- textures;
- audio samples;
- middleware;
- plugins;
- stock assets;
- outsourced artwork;
- mocap/voice agreements;
- AI-assisted assets if used.

Do not ship placeholder assets whose commercial rights are unclear.

## Contractor agreements

Every contractor contributing code, art, music, writing or voice should have agreements covering:

- scope;
- payment;
- ownership/license transfer;
- portfolio rights;
- confidentiality where needed;
- warranties regarding third-party infringement.

## Originality review gates

Conduct review at:

- concept art lock;
- vertical slice;
- first public trailer;
- content-complete alpha;
- pre-release marketing asset review.

Invite reviewers unfamiliar with internal intent; ask what existing game/character each element reminds them of.

## Documentation rule

When a design choice is intentionally inspired by a genre convention, record the **player need** rather than “copy X feature.”

Example:

Bad: “Use Hollow Knight benches but make them machines.”

Good: “Players need predictable safe nodes for saving, loadout editing and fast travel; implement them as Meridian Relays integrated into the infrastructure.”

That framing encourages independent solutions.
