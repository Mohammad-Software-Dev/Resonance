# Enemies, Bosses and Encounter Design

## Enemy design grammar

Enemies exist to create movement and Resonance problems.

Each enemy should answer:

- What space does it control?
- What does it make the player notice?
- What Resonance interaction does it teach/reward?
- How does it combine with another role?

## Enemy families

### Drift fauna

Adapted to unstable gravity.

Examples:

- **Skimmer:** arcs between local gravity seams; teaches aerial timing.
- **Latchwing:** attaches to anchors and temporarily disrupts them.
- **Podback:** armored herbivore whose shell can be removed and thrown.

### Maintenance constructs

Old machines following corrupted/incomplete routines.

- **Clamp:** anchors itself and fires tether lines.
- **Surveyor:** marks player location for remote hazards.
- **Hauler:** heavy movable machine carrying Resonant cargo.
- **Mirror Warden:** reflects frontal projectile manipulation, encouraging repositioning.

### Rust Sea organisms

Life using metallic particles.

- **Dredger:** hides under particle surface, detectable by Resonance response.
- **Cloudmouth:** gathers loose objects into protective shell.
- **Needle Current:** schooling hazard that behaves like a moving field rather than individual enemies.

### Resonant manifestations

Semi-physical entities from the field network.

- cannot always be hit conventionally;
- require Phase, Invert or environment interaction;
- attacks emphasize pattern and signal.

## Encounter composition

Small combat spaces should usually use 2–4 complementary roles rather than large homogeneous swarms.

Example:

- Surveyor marks player.
- Clamp denies a useful anchor.
- Two Skimmers pressure airspace.

The player can solve by killing threats or using Resonance to change geometry.

## Multiplayer scaling

1 player:

- fewer simultaneous attack vectors;
- Echo-friendly timing windows.

2 players:

- coordinated enemy pair behaviors;
- pressure may split between players;
- more opportunities for team throws/links.

3–4 players:

- additional lanes/objectives;
- support enemies become more important;
- selected elites gain multi-target patterns;
- modest health increases only.

## Boss design principles

A major boss should have:

1. Strong visual concept.
2. Clear relationship to its region.
3. At least one Resonance interaction learned previously.
4. A phase that transforms movement assumptions.
5. Multiplayer adaptation beyond health.
6. Solo Echo compatibility where simultaneous actors are required.
7. Short retry loop.

## Example major bosses

### The Pendulum

Region: Gravity Orchard.

Concept: enormous agricultural maintenance engine suspended from three Resonant pivots.

Phase 1:

- attacks while hanging above intact floor;
- player learns to use moving limb as anchor.

Phase 2:

- floor sections collapse;
- traversal becomes orbit-focused;
- damaged armor plates become temporary platforms/objects.

Phase 3:

- removing armor makes boss vulnerable but also removes safe anchors, creating a self-escalating arena.

Multiplayer:

- different players can control/strain separate pivots.

Solo:

- Echo maintains one strain while player attacks another.

### Astral Shepherd

Region: Transit/Foundry boundary.

Concept: multi-armed station guardian designed to align ring segments.

Core mechanic: opposing force.

Players loosen arm joints, then apply force from opposite sides to break configuration locks.

Multiplayer adds additional simultaneous limbs/patterns rather than only health.

### Choir Prime

Region: Choir Array.

Concept: boss exists partly as sound/field pattern.

Players redirect signal through arena towers. Attacking the visible body alone is ineffective.

Phase changes rhythm and target logic; accessibility includes visual timing cues independent of hearing.

### The Glass Regent

Region: Solar Foundry.

Concept: autonomous fabrication intelligence housed in shifting mirrored shells.

Mechanics:

- redirects beams;
- creates brittle glass armor;
- players can pull shards into orbit and repel them through exposed nodes.

Co-op supports passing a captured shard between players for increased momentum.

### Leviathan of the Flood

Region: Flooded Observatory.

A creature that evolved inside free-floating water masses.

Fight alternates between water sphere interiors and dry architecture. Players manipulate entire water volumes to expose or redirect it.

### Dust Cathedral

Region: Rust Sea.

Not a single creature: a colony using millions of metal particles to construct temporary bodies.

Players strip away particle mass, reveal control knots, and survive changing terrain as the “boss” consumes the arena.

### Loom Harvester

Region: Cloud Loom.

Long exterior chase/fight where the boss and players move across industrial kites. High emphasis on launch/catch.

### Meridian Custodian

Late-game rival/guardian whose Resonance toolset mirrors player fundamentals but applies them at infrastructural scale.

Should test mastery without becoming a direct player-character duel clone.

### Tessel Configuration / Core Encounter

Endgame encounter varies by narrative path. Could be combat, stabilization trial or hybrid depending on choices.

## Mini-boss philosophy

Mini-bosses are often advanced versions of enemy-system interactions:

- elite armored Hauler in moving freight room;
- two linked constructs that share stability;
- predator that steals/occupies anchors;
- field manifestation fought across phase states.

They should prepare players for regional Guardians.

## Boss checkpointing

- Relay or direct restart immediately outside arena.
- Loadout changes allowed.
- Intro skippable after first attempt.
- Multiplayer reconnect/regroup supported before restart.

## Telegraphing

Each dangerous attack communicates:

- source;
- area/direction;
- timing;
- whether Resonance can interact.

Do not rely on color alone. Use motion, shape and audio layers.

## Boss mastery rewards

Reward types:

- major ability;
- world transformation;
- Core technique;
- route unlock;
- narrative consequence.

Avoid bosses whose only payoff is generic currency.
